## Username + bio

Your profile is the landing page. People decide in seconds whether to follow, save, or act.

### Username

- Use your **real brand name** — searchable, recognizable, consistent with signage and Google Maps.
- Avoid random numbers or underscores unless needed for availability.
- Keep it **short and easy to spell** so guests can find you after word-of-mouth.
- Match handle across platforms (Instagram, Google, reservation tools) when possible.

### Display name

- Include **cuisine + neighborhood or city** if space allows (e.g. "Luna Trattoria · Mitte").
- Display name is searchable; username alone is not always enough for discovery.

### Profile photo

- Logo or a **clear, recognizable food/venue shot** — readable at thumbnail size.
- Avoid busy backgrounds or text-heavy images.

### Category

- Set the correct business category (**Restaurant**, **Cafe**, **Bar**, etc.) so Instagram surfaces you for local intent.

### Bio (4 lines — make every line count)

1. **What you are** — cuisine, vibe, or signature promise (not generic filler).
2. **Where you are** — neighborhood, landmark, or city (local relevance).
3. **Why visit** — one proof point (award, specialty dish, years open, chef-led).
4. **What to do next** — one clear CTA: Reserve · Order · Walk in · DM for events.

**Bio examples (patterns, not copy-paste):**

- "Neapolitan pizza · Kreuzberg · wood-fired since 2019 · Book a table ↓"
- "Modern Vietnamese · lunch & dinner · vegan options · Order for pickup ↓"

### Bio link

- One link-in-bio destination with **1–3 priority actions** (reserve, order, menu).
- Use UTM tags on links so you can measure what Instagram drives.

### Contact buttons

- Enable **call, email, and directions** — remove friction for high-intent guests.

### Pinned posts (3)

Pin your strongest evergreen content:

1. Signature dish or hero experience
2. Social proof (reviews, press, guest moments)
3. How to visit (location, hours, how to book/order)

## When to use Reels

**Default rule:** use Reels when the goal is **reach, discovery, and attention** — especially from people who do not follow you yet.

Instagram pushes Reels to non-followers through Reels tab, Explore, and recommendations. For restaurants, that means new locals, tourists, and "where should we eat?" browsers.

### Use Reels for

- **Discovery** — getting in front of new audiences near your location
- **Appetite appeal** — sizzling, pouring, plating, steam, texture (food that looks alive)
- **Behind-the-scenes** — kitchen prep, sourcing, service moments, team personality
- **Atmosphere** — vibe of the room, terrace, bar, event energy
- **Quick tips** — how to order, what to try first, pairing ideas
- **Trends** — only when they fit your brand; forced trends hurt trust
- **Staff and culture** — human, authentic moments (not over-produced ads)

### Reel format basics

- **9:16 vertical** (1080 × 1920)
- **Hook in the first 1–2 seconds** — strongest visual or line upfront
- **Design for sound-off** — subtitles or on-screen text
- **One clear idea per Reel** — do not cram a full menu into 15 seconds

### Do not default to Reels for

- Detailed menus or long price lists (use carousel posts)
- Offers that need fine print, terms, or date restrictions (pair Reel with Story or post)
- Content that only existing regulars need (Stories or Highlights may be enough)

### Cadence baseline

- Small team: **1–2 Reels per week**
- Larger team: **3–5 Reels per week**

## When to use Stories

**Default rule:** use Stories to build a **personal connection** with people who already follow you — the informal, human layer of your account.

Stories feel closer and less polished than the feed. Guests follow restaurants they like; Stories are where you stay present in their day without always selling. A quick "happy weekend", "good luck with the new week", thank-you after a busy service, or a casual hello from the team often outperforms another promo slide because it feels real.

Stories disappear after 24 hours (unless saved to Highlights). That makes them ideal for **in-the-moment** updates — relational, timely, and low-friction — not permanent reference content.

### Use Stories for

- **Personal connection** — wish a happy weekend, good start to the week, holiday greetings, or thank guests after a busy night; show the people behind the restaurant, not only the menu
- **Today's specials** — soup of the day, market fish, sold-out warnings
- **Limited-time offers** — lunch deal, happy hour, event tickets
- **Real-time availability** — "walk-ins welcome tonight", "fully booked — join waitlist"
- **Polls and questions** — "Which dessert should we bring back?", dietary FAQs
- **Countdowns** — new menu launch, holiday brunch, collaboration dinner
- **Behind-the-scenes today** — prep starting, bread out of the oven, terrace open
- **Resharing** — repost guest tags, UGC, and your own new Reels/posts with a direct CTA sticker
- **Reservation and order nudges** — link sticker to book, order, or event page

### Story features that drive action

- **Link sticker** — reserve, order, menu, event signup
- **Poll / question / slider** — quick engagement and menu feedback
- **Countdown sticker** — launches and limited windows
- **Add Yours** — encourage guests to share their visit (UGC pipeline)

### Cadence baseline

- **Daily Stories** during service days — even 2–4 frames is enough
- Mix **relational** frames (greetings, team moments, casual updates) with **action** frames (specials, offers, links) — not every Story needs a CTA
- Post when you have something **worth sharing now**; do not spam empty frames

### Do not rely on Stories alone for

- Evergreen info (hours, address, full menu) — save to **Highlights**
- Long storytelling — use carousel posts or Reels
- Content you want searchable months later — feed posts and Highlights

## When to use Posts

**Default rule:** use feed **Posts** when content needs to **stay on your profile**, build **trust**, or reward **deeper attention** (reading, swiping, saving).

Posts are your permanent storefront. New visitors scroll your grid to decide if you are worth a visit.

### Use a single-image Post for

- One strong dish, room shot, or announcement understood at a glance
- Simple openings, awards, or "we're hiring" messages
- Clean brand moments where one frame is enough

### Use a Carousel Post for

- **Menus** and seasonal launches (slide by slide)
- **Step-by-step** — how a dish is made, tasting menu flow
- **Multiple dishes** — "5 things to order on your first visit"
- **Before/after** — renovation, recipe development, plating evolution
- **Educational content** — wine pairings, ingredient stories, allergen info
- **Social proof** — review screenshots, press quotes, guest photo sets

Carousels often earn more **saves and time-on-post** — strong signals for consideration.

### Use Posts (not only Reels) for

- **Promotions with context** — always explain _why_ and _when_ (see Do's and Don'ts)
- **Brand-building** — chef story, sourcing, values, team introductions
- **Customer reviews and UGC** — proof that real people love you
- **Event and private dining** — details guests will look up later

### Caption framework

**Hook → Context → Proof → CTA**

Example: "The weekday lunch ready in 12 minutes. Built for nearby offices. Top 3 reorders this month. Reserve or DM for group orders."

### Posting hygiene

- **Location tag** on local-intent content
- **Alt text** on images — accessibility and clarity
- **Meaningful captions** — dish, cuisine, occasion; not hashtag dumps
- **One clear CTA per post** — reserve, order, walk in, DM

### Cadence baseline

- Small team: **3–4 feed posts per week**
- Larger team: **5–7 feed posts per week**

### Format-to-goal cheat sheet

| Goal                                     | Best format             |
| ---------------------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| New people discover you                  | Reel                    |
| Guest saves for later / compares options | Carousel post           |
| Guest acts today                         | Story                   |
| Guest looks up info anytime              | Highlight + pinned post |

## What to put on Highlights

**Default rule:** Highlights are your **evergreen navigation** — the answers guests ask before they visit.

Think of Highlights as a mini-website on your profile: menu, how to get there, how to book, proof, and FAQs.

### Recommended baseline Highlights

| Highlight            | What to include                                                            |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Menu**             | Signature dishes, categories, seasonal features, dietary icons (V, VG, GF) |
| **Reserve / Order**  | How to book, order links, delivery platforms, group booking notes          |
| **Location & hours** | Address, map pin, parking/transit, opening hours, holiday exceptions       |
| **Reviews / UGC**    | Guest photos, review quotes, press mentions, reshare-worthy tags           |
| **Events**           | Private dining, catering, live music, brunch, seasonal pop-ups             |
| **FAQ**              | Allergens, kids, dogs, dress code, peak times, payment, large groups       |

### Optional Highlights (add when relevant)

- **Behind the scenes** — kitchen, suppliers, team (evergreen best-of, not daily clutter)
- **Drinks / Wine** — cocktail list, wine focus, happy hour
- **New** — rolling window for latest launch; archive or trim when stale
- **About** — story, chef, values, sustainability

### Highlight cover tips

- Use **consistent cover icons or brand colors** so the row looks intentional.
- Short, obvious titles — guests should know what they will get before tapping.

### What not to put in Highlights

- Expired promos ("20% off last March")
- Low-quality or off-brand Story dumps
- Duplicate of everything on your website with no visual value

Refresh Highlights when menu, hours, or booking flow changes.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Promotions and discounts

- **Do** always add context and a reason — guests trust offers that feel intentional, not desperate.
  - Good: "Lunch offer Mon–Fri, 11:00–14:00 — 10% off mains for nearby offices."
  - Bad: "10% OFF!!!" with no time, day, or item scope.
- **Do** keep offers **margin-safe** and easy for staff to explain at the door.
- **Do** use **time bounds** — daypart, date range, dine-in vs takeaway.
- **Don't** train your audience to only visit on discount — balance promo with trust and appetite content.

### Content mix (starter ratio)

- **40%** trust and brand (team, story, proof)
- **40%** appetite and engagement (dishes, BTS, atmosphere)
- **20%** conversion and promotion (offers, events, book now)

Adjust after 4–6 weeks using saves, shares, profile visits, and DMs.

### Engagement and community

- **Do** reply to comments within **12–24 hours** and DMs within **1–6 hours** on service days.
- **Do** treat **saves and shares** as stronger intent signals than likes alone.
- **Don't** use engagement pods, fake comments, or bought followers — risks account health and skews data.

### Discovery (2026 defaults)

- Discovery is **recommendation-driven** — Reels and strong creative reach non-followers.
- **Hashtags support** discovery but are not the core strategy; captions, location, and profile context matter more.
- There is no universal **best posting time** — test your local lunch, dinner, and weekend patterns.

### Measurement basics

Track weekly: reach, saves, shares, profile visits, DM starts.  
Track monthly: reservations, walk-ins, orders, repeat visits.  
Use UTMs, unique codes, and "how did you hear about us?" at the host stand or checkout.
