# Restaurant marketing psychology

Use these guest-decision principles when advising on menu features, Instagram,
pricing tests, and weak-item messaging. Load chart data for which dishes, slots,
or combos to apply them to — never invent venue sales figures.

## Feature what sells (and why guests choose)

- **Jobs to be done**: frame dishes by the occasion (quick lunch, shareable
  night out, comfort order) — not only ingredients.
- **Mere exposure**: repeat stars / plow horses across posts and dayparts so
  familiarity builds preference; respect opening hours for posting times.
- **Social proof**: show real crowd moments, bestsellers, and “guests keep
  ordering…” — only when chart or operator evidence supports it.
- **Mimetic desire**: limited features and waitlist-style drops work when the
  item is genuinely scarce or seasonal — not fake urgency.

## Reduce friction to order

- **Paradox of choice / Hick’s law**: highlight one clear hero per post or
  daypart; avoid dumping the whole menu in one creative.
- **Activation energy**: make the next step obvious (order / reserve / visit
  tonight) with a single CTA.
- **Peak-end rule**: design posts around a memorable peak (plating, combo
  reveal) and a strong close (hours, offer, or substitute dish).

## Price, cut, and rotate carefully

- **Anchoring & framing**: when testing price, show the anchor honestly
  (prior price, portion, or combo context); prefer “reprice / improve /
  remove” language from weak-item playbooks over vague discounts.
- **Loss aversion**: “don’t miss tonight’s specialty” beats vague future ROI —
  only when the specialty is real.
- **Status-quo bias**: removals feel risky; pair cuts with a clear substitute
  from stars / plow horses so guests are not left with a blank slot.
- **Sunk cost**: past kitchen investment does not justify keeping a weak
  item that charts show as underperforming — decide from current sales data.

## Second-order effects

- Flash sales can train guests to wait for deals; prefer durable menu and
  content moves grounded in heatmaps, pair lift, and venue slots.
- Psychology explains _how_ to message; charts decide _what_ to promote.
